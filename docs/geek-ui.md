# The geek theme

The geek theme is a keyboard-first interface designed like a code editor: no rounded zones, no scroll bars, no animations, a compact status bar, and menus in the style of [Helix](https://helix-editor.com). The specification (in French) is `specifications/ui.md`.

The app always opens in this theme.

## Layout

- **Mail list** on the left (about a third of the window; drag the separator to resize), **reading pane** on the right. Each row: the unread dot, the date, the sender, a **paperclip** when the conversation has attachments, the subject and its tags, (in Everything) the folder, the number of messages, the **flag**. The sender column names the **person the conversation is with**, not the author of its latest mail: once the user has answered, the latest author is the user, which said nothing. It shows the latest author who is not the user, or, for a mail the user wrote alone, its first recipient (To before Cc). A muted **→** before the name says the user wrote the latest mail, and the conversation waits on the other person: "→ Alice" (added in November 2026; VoiceOver reads "Alice, you wrote last"). An unsent draft does not count: a reply being written leaves the row as it was. The core decides who is the user (`correspondent`, `last_is_mine` of `list_threads`, `docs/core-library.md`); the app only draws it (`SenderColumn`). The paperclip has no number and comes before the subject: next to the message count at the end of the row, "📎 3" would read as three attachments. The flag is an orange icon at the end of the row, a colour of its own, apart from the unread dot's (`ThreadRow`, `RowLayout`: fixed icon columns, so the rows line up). ⌘A marks every mail of the list as shown (the rows loaded so far), for a grouped action; the status bar says how many, Escape unmarks. Only on the mail screen, without a panel open.
- **Two presentations of the list** (added in November 2026): `v` `l` (Show, "list: one line or three per mail") goes from one to the other, and the status bar says which. **One line per mail**, in columns (above), is the default of geek; clean and regular open on three lines (`list_layout` of the theme's `[window]`, [themes.md](themes.md)). **Three lines per mail**, as Apple Mail, lets the list column be much narrower: on the first line the unread dot, the person (always bold, as in Apple Mail: it heads each mail; the dot alone says unread) and, on the right, the paperclip, the flag and the date; on the second the subject and its tags, then on the right (in Everything) the folder, the AI's suggestion and the number of messages; on the third the beginning of the latest message, muted, on one line (line breaks become spaces; a mail without one keeps an empty line, so that every row has the same height and `ctrl-d` / `ctrl-u` count the mails that fit). Once chosen with `v` `l`, the presentation is remembered by the app, as the number of columns, and holds in every folder and every theme. Each presentation keeps its own width of the list: a third of the window in columns, a quarter on three lines, until you drag the separator, and the width you give is kept for that presentation only (`ListLayout`, `StackedThreadRow`).
- `v1` shows the list only: Return, or a double-click on a row, reads the mail in the whole window and marks it read at once, `j` / `k` go on to the next / previous mail, and Escape goes back to the list. `v2` shows the list and the mail, `v3` adds the folders on the left, in a single block titled with the current view: **all** or the account's name.

## Window

The title bar of the window (its title and the red, orange and green buttons) is shown by default. The theme says it (`title_bar` in `[window]`, [themes.md](themes.md)); `ui.title_bar` of `config.toml`, when written, still wins. Hidden, the content goes up to the top edge, and the status bar, at the top by default, takes its place; its empty parts move the window when dragged and zoom it when double-clicked. Everything else of a window stays: resize by the edges, ⌘W, ⌘M, ⌘Q, full screen with ⌃⌘F. The app has a **single window**: there is no File > New Window (⌘N) and no window tabs. Its windows would all show the same folder, selection and reply, since they share one model; a second window becomes possible once each has a context of its own (`specifications/ui.md`, "Fenêtres"; `SingleWindow`).

The app applies a change of the file at once (`config_status` gives it, `ConfigChanged` says to read it again). With the status bar hidden (`vs`) or at the bottom and the title bar hidden, the window is moved by the bottom bar, or not at all when no bar is shown: show the title bar, or the status bar, to move it.

The keys of the app are commands in this window only. In the Preferences, the open and save panels and any other window, they go to that window: a letter typed in a field is text (`Interface/KeyRouting.swift`).

## Accounts and the unified view "all"

With several accounts, the app opens on **all**, the unified view: each folder (Inbox, Archive, Todo…) gathers the folders of the same role in every account, with their unread counts added up, and lists their mails together, newest first. Choose another view with `@` (or a click on the account in the status bar or on the title of the folder column): **all** comes first, then each account. Each row shows its unread count at the right end, as in the `;` menu. Changing view keeps the folder role (Archive to Archive), or falls back to the Inbox. With a single account there is nothing to choose: the account block leaves the status bar, `@` and a click on the title of the folder column do nothing, and the help leaves the key out.

The `;` menu and the folder column only show the folders of the current view: the account is chosen with `@`. The `;` menu and the folder column show four blocks, each ended by a virtual folder that lists folders of the view together (all accounts in "all"): Inbox, Todo, Waiting, then **Work** (`;o`, those three); Archive, Newsletter, Paperwork, Quarantine; Sent, Drafts, then **All Mail** (`;m`, every folder but Deleted, Junk and Banned, Quarantine included, what most searches want); Deleted, Junk, Banned, then **Everything** (`;e`, every folder). The virtual folders are drawn apart from the real ones: a stack icon before the name, in the accent colour (`VirtualFolderLabel`). A conversation present in several folders (Inbox and Sent) appears once, in the folder of its latest mail; in All Mail, a conversation whose latest mail was deleted keeps its place, that of its latest mail left there. Since the list mixes every folder, each row shows its folder in a muted column before the message count, and the reader shows it at the right end of the subject line. In "all", the account follows the folder ("Archive · perso"): always in the reader, and in the list only when the subject keeps at least 30 characters (a wide list, one column), otherwise the folder alone. Moves and other actions apply to each mail in its own account.
- The **status bar** shows the account (`@` icon, with several accounts only) and the folder (folder icon), each followed by the key that opens it: `@` for the account picker, `;` for the menu of folders and screens. A click on either opens the same; the rows of a menu can be clicked too. A second click on the block of the menu or picker open closes it, and so does a click on an empty part of the bar (the part that moves the window), as a click on the mail does. While a tag, group or contact is listed, it shows that instead. Messages (result of an action, errors) appear on the right and disappear at the next key. While an account is being synchronized for the first time, the right end also says how far it is, beside the messages: "Importing Inbox: 12,300 of 44,829", then "Downloading mails: 41,429 left" ([sync.md](sync.md), "Progress"). A move names the mail and the folder: “Devis toiture” moved to Archive (the subject is cut at 50 characters); several mails give their number instead: 3 threads moved to Archive. At its right end, a `?` block (a question mark in a circle) opens the list of keys, as the `?` key does; a second click closes it. `vs` moves it to the top, hides it, or brings it back to the bottom.
- When a list opens, its first mail is selected, so the reading pane always shows something.
- When the selected mail leaves the list (moved, read in a list of unread mails), the cursor goes to the next mail **still in the list**. A change can take several mails at once: moving a mail to Banned, Newsletter or Paperwork also files the other mails of its sender, which may include the one just below. The cursor then skips them all and goes on to the first remaining mail after them (or the nearest one before, at the end of the list), never back to the top (`AppModel.successor`).

Layout choices (columns, status bar position, list width) are remembered.

## Text size

Two sizes, set in `config.toml` and applied at once when the file changes:

- `ui.text_size` (default 16 pt): the whole window but the status bar. Everything follows it: fonts, row heights, column widths, menus, pickers and the HTML view.
- `ui.status_bar_text_size` (default 18 pt): the status bar alone, its blocks, chips, messages and the line typed there; its height follows it too.

Both go from 9 to 32 points; a value outside is refused on its line ([configuration.md](configuration.md)). The lengths of the layout are written for 12 pt and scaled from there (`TextSize.referenceSize`).

As in Zed, **⌘+** (or **⌘=**, which needs no Shift on AZERTY keyboards) makes both sizes one point bigger, **⌘−** one point smaller, and **⌘0** brings them back to the values of the file. The same commands are in the View menu, and both ways say the size reached in the status bar ("Text size: 16 pt", and "…, status bar 12 pt" when the two differ). This step lasts for the session only: the file is the one place that says the size, so a size remembered by the app can never silently win over an edit of the file. An edit of either size in the file also clears the step; an edit of another key keeps it.

## Tags and groups in the header

After the subject, the title line of the open mail shows the conversation's tags (`#Urgent`, as in the list) and the groups of the person it is with (`@VIP`, as in the folder column), in the accent colour (`ReaderTitleBar`). The person is the one the sender column names and `g` adds, so the header names exactly the groups `g` can remove them from. When the subject does not fit beside them, the labels start the second line, before the controls; they are never cut. The tooltip says what they are and that `t` and `g` change them. The core sends them with the thread (`tags`, `correspondent`, `correspondent_groups` of `get_thread`). The open thread is read again after a tag changes (the core's `ThreadChanged`) and after `g` (no event says a membership changed).

## Adding and removing tags and groups

`t` and `g` give each action its own row, for the current mail or the marked ones (`LabelChoices`). Removal rows come first, marked `−`: "remove Urgent" for a tag at least one of the mails has, or a group the person of at least one of them is in; removing only touches the mails that have it. Then the rows that add: the existing names not every mail has yet. A name some of the marked mails have therefore gets both rows, and with several mails each row says how many have it ("1 of 2"). A name typed that does not exist gives the `+` row, which creates it. Names compare without case, as in the core. For one mail, this is simply its tags (or its person's groups) to remove, then the others to add.

The groups of the marked mails' people come from the core when `g` opens (`thread_labels`, 1,000 threads at most); until it answers, the header's groups stand for the open mail, so its removal rows show at once. Before (November 2026), `t` on a tag every mail had silently removed it, and `g` only added.

## Groups in the folder column

In the three-column layout (`v3`), the contact groups are listed under the folders, after a "Groups" title (`ColumnGroups`). A click on a group narrows the **current list** (the folder, the unified folder, Everything, a tag's list…) to the mails of its members: it adds the group to the Group… attribute, with its chip in the status bar; a second click removes it. Several groups are OR-ed. ⌘-click excludes the group instead ("not group: …"). A group that filters the list is marked `•` (excluded: `−`, struck through) in the accent colour; the others show `@`. Its row also has the grey background of the current folder (`InterfaceController.isHighlighted`), whether it was chosen by a click or in the Group… panel of `f`, wanted or excluded: the folder and the active groups together say what the list shows. Like every attribute, the groups stay when the folder changes, and Escape removes them. No group, no block.

The column shows eight groups at most (`GroupRanking.columnLimit`). With more, it keeps the most relevant ones and ends with "N more…", which opens the Group… panel listing them all (the "Groups" title opens it too). Relevance takes two signals from `list_groups`: the latest change of the group or of its members (`date_modified`), and the mails received from its members (`mail_count`). The two rankings are interleaved: the most recent group, then the biggest one not taken yet, then the next most recent, and so on, ties kept in display order. A score mixing a date and a count would need weights with no natural value; the interleaving needs none, and a new group shows at once while a big old one stays. The groups filtering the list are always shown, even past the limit. The rows keep the display order (the order the groups were made in), so a group never jumps to another row, only in or out. The groups are read again with the folders (at each change the core announces) and after `g`; the core has no group event yet.

## Attributes (filters)

The magnifier after the folder in the status bar, named **Filter** when the theme names its blocks (key **f**, or a click), opens the attribute selector, whose panel is titled Filter too. Its rows go in pairs, a thin, discreet line between two pairs, each row starting with a capital (November 2026; `ThreadFilter.selectorLayout`): Unread / Read, Flagged / Not flagged, From… / To…, Subject… / Text…, Conversation date… / Mail date…, Group… / Tag…, Human / Robot, With attachments / Without attachments, With my reply / Without my reply, Conversation / Single mail, then "Clear all attributes" when one is on. A pair is an attribute and its opposite, or two of a kind. Once something is typed, the rows are ranked and the lines go. The chips keep the lower case ("unread"). The selector is as tall as all its rows, "clear" included, at any text size (`PickerPopup.height`): its rows are few and fixed, and one that had to be scrolled to went unnoticed (Mail date… and "clear" were below the edge until November 2026). Only a window too short makes it smaller; its rows then scroll. Attributes narrow the current list (folder of an account, or of "all", or the mails of a tag, a group or a contact, whose status bar then shows the magnifier and the chips after "tag: …"), combined with AND; a thread qualifies when its mails in the listed folders match (in Everything, every folder: a thread shown in Sent because your reply is its latest mail is still seen with the mail it answers).

### Reaching a row from the keyboard

The selector opens on its first row and Return chooses the highlighted row, so what matters is which row comes first once you type. Rows are ranked (`PickerMatch.swift`), best first; rows of the same rank keep the list order:

1. the label starts with the typed text: `read` puts "read" first, before "unread" which only contains it; `lu` puts "lus" before "non lus";
2. each typed word starts a word of the label, in any order: `lus` finds "non lus", `jointe sans` finds "sans pièce jointe";
3. the typed letters are the initials of the label's words (one word of two letters or more): `nf` → "not flagged", `sd` → "sans drapeau", `spj` → "sans pièce jointe", `sm` → "sans ma réponse";
4. each typed word appears anywhere in the label (the former filter, so nothing it found is lost): `lagg` → "flagged".

Case and accents are ignored (`a` finds "À…"). Attributes with a value also have hidden search words, localized (`AttributeField.keywords`): `cc` or `destinataire` finds To… / À…, `expéditeur` or `sender` finds From… / De…. The French labels were chosen so that two letters always reach a row: "De…", "Date conversation…" and "drapeau" differ at the second letter (`da` reaches Date conversation…, listed before Date mail…, which `dm` reaches), and "À…" replaces "Destinataire…", which started like "De…" (and matches the "à :" chip and the "À :" header of a mail).

### Typed dates

The input line of every value panel says what can be typed while nothing is typed (in Date…, which opens on its presets, it was otherwise invisible). The Date… panel takes dates and durations:

- `2026-10-01` anywhere; with slashes or dots, in the order of the Mac's region (`DateChoice.Order`, from the region's short date): `1/10/2026`, `1/10/26`, `1.10.2026` in France, `10/1/2026` in the United States, `2026/10/1` in Japan;
- **six digits**, read first in the region's order (`ddmmyy`, `mmddyy` or `yymmdd`), then as `yymmdd` (as `ddmmyy` where the year comes first). The panel's hint gives its examples in that order. Each reading that is a real day gives two rows, "after" and "before" that day. When both readings are real days, the panel shows four rows, and each says on its right how the digits were read ("dd mm yy", "yy mm dd"): `010120` is 1 January 2020 or 20 January 2001. One real day gives two rows (`311299`: 31 December 1999; `290223`: 23 February 2029, since 2023 has no 29 February). Both readings on the same day count once (`111111`). Two-digit years below 70 are 20yy, from 70 19yy.

- **a duration**: a number and a unit, by its first letter in English or in French, or its whole word: `d`/`j` day, `w`/`s` week, `m` month, `y`/`a` year (`10j`, `3 s`, `8w`, `6m`, `2 years`). Both languages are understood whatever the app's language, since the letters do not collide. No number means 1 (`m` is one month). The panel offers "newer than 10 days" (French "depuis 10 jours") and "older than 10 days" ("il y a plus de 10 jours"), with on the right the date it gives. Every unit counts back from now by the calendar: `1d` is this time yesterday, `1m` this day last month, so `7d` and `1w` agree.

"after" a day includes that day; "before" it does not.

**Two bounds, an interval.** Each row sets one bound, the lower one ("after", "newer than", the presets but the last) or the upper one ("before", "older than", "older than a year"), and keeps the other: "after 2020-01-01" then "before 2020-07-01" is the first half of 2020, shown as two chips, each removable alone. Choosing a bound again replaces it. A row that would leave no day between the two bounds says "nothing in between" on its right; it can still be chosen (the list is then empty), since the next choice usually moves the other bound.

### Values: domains and exclusions

In the From… and To… panels, a value starting with `@` is a **domain**: `@acme.fr` matches `jean@acme.fr` and its subdomains (`x@mail.acme.fr`), not `x@notacme.fr`; the row says "domain" on its right. Without `@`, a value is any part of a name or address (`acme.fr` also finds `notacme.fr`).

Choosing Text… or Subject… again opens its panel on the current words, selected, as in a macOS field reached with Tab: typing (or ⌘V) replaces them, Return alone keeps them, → (or ←) ends the selection to edit them, for instance to add `-word` after the fact, and Delete then Return removes the criterion ("remove subject: …"). A second Subject… search therefore replaces the first one instead of narrowing it unseen. Text… never looks at the addresses: searching a name in the text does not bring back every mail of that person; From… and To… do that.

### Negation

- **Yes / no attributes**: each has its opposite as a row of its own ("sans drapeau", "sans pièce jointe", "non lus", "sans ma réponse", "mails seuls"), reached by its initials (`sd`, `spj`, `nl`, `sm`). Choosing the opposite of an active value replaces it.
- **From…, To…, Text…, Subject…**: the panel shows the typed value ("+", Return) and, just under it, the same value excluded ("− not …", Down then Return). Typing the value with a leading `-` (`-@acme.fr`) leaves only the exclusion, Return at once; the contacts suggested are then offered as exclusions too.
- In Text… and Subject…, words can be mixed: `renouvellement -facture` wants one word and excludes the other. An exclusion added later joins the words already there.
- **Combination**: the wanted values of an attribute are OR-ed, its excluded values AND-ed ("from alice or bob, but from nobody at acme.fr"); a thread is left out as soon as one of its mails in the listed folders matches an excluded value. A value is wanted or excluded, not both: the last choice wins.
- **Chips**: `not from: @acme.fr`, `not to: @acme.fr`; Text… and Subject… keep the dash of their excluded words (`text: renouvellement -facture`).

Example, at a registrar: the mails that talk about the domain acme.fr, without those sent to or by acme.fr. `f` `te` Return `acme.fr` Return; `f` `de` Return `-@acme.fr` Return; `f` `a` Return `-@acme.fr` Return (French keys; in English `fr` and `to` instead of `de` and `a`). Three chips: `text: acme.fr`, `not from: @acme.fr`, `not to: @acme.fr`.

## Search (`/`)

`/` is the widest search, the joker: its words are looked for anywhere, subject, sender, recipients and body (where Text… leaves the addresses out), and `-word` excludes a word there too. It opens a panel titled "Search everywhere"; Return goes to **All Mail** of the current view (every folder but Deleted, Junk and Banned, every account in "all") with these words; typed from Everything, it stays in Everything. The result is the ordinary list: one row per conversation, with its folder, the `anywhere:` chip, and `f` to narrow it further (unread, From…). Escape clears the attributes and goes back to the folder `/` was pressed from; going elsewhere first (`;`, `@`, a click on a folder) forgets that folder, and Escape then only clears the attributes. A second `/` replaces the words and keeps the folder to go back to.

## Reading a mail

The whole theme (lists, status bar, menus, mails, including the HTML view, where a font the sender names wins in its own text) uses **iA Writer Mono V**, a font bundled with the app (SIL Open Font License). List columns have fixed widths counted in characters of this monospaced font, computed from the list width, so they stay aligned when the window is resized. Mails are shown as plain text in geek (HTML in clean and regular: `reader.body_format` of the theme, [themes.md](themes.md)), simplified: each message shows what it brings, the history it repeats is hidden, older read messages are folded (see `thread-display.md`). The `text | html | images` switch at the top of the mail, or `h` (also `v` `h`), shows the sanitized HTML version instead (scripts are removed by the core, and remote images are not loaded; the sender's CSS, layout and embedded images are kept, each mail in a frame of its own, a mail made for a white page adapted to the dark theme by Dark Reader: see `thread-display.md`, "HTML of the sender"); `images`, or `vi`, also loads its remote images (see "Remote images" below). Each word of the switch is clicked on its own. The **title line** holds the subject, then the controls of the mail: first the information (the folder in "Everything", `T`, the blocked pixels, "unsubscribed on …"), then the buttons (unsubscribe, the flag, the switch, which always ends the line). Aligned on the right, the buttons thus stay at the same place from one mail to the next, whatever the length of the folder's name. It stays on one line while the whole subject fits beside them; otherwise the subject takes its own line and the controls go on a second one, aligned on the right. The choice is measured, not set by a threshold: the length of the subject, the text size (⌘+ ⌘−), the controls the mail has and the width of the reader all count, and it is made again when one changes (`ReaderTitleBar`). On two lines, a subject longer than the whole reader is cut at the end, its tooltip shows it whole. The choice is remembered **for the sender**, always as an explicit value: the next mails from the same address open the same way, in every theme.

### Senders in the address book

The links panel (`l`) looks up the **senders of the conversation** in the Mac's address book (Contacts): the authors of its received messages, latest first; not the user's own messages (Sent, Drafts), nor the people in copy. For each card found: one row per phone number, named after the person and the card's label (`Alice Martin — mobile`, the number as the card writes it on the right), then **Card of Alice Martin**, which Return opens in Contacts. Return on a number calls it, as for the numbers of the mail. A number that is also in the mail (a signature) is listed once, under the name; a number shared by two cards (a switchboard), once under the first.

- **Sender in Contacts**: at the end of the line of each unfolded message (after the address), an icon says whether its sender is in the address book: a closed book when a card holds the address (whatever its case), a person with a question mark when none does. The user's own messages (Sent, Drafts) have none, nor does any message while the book cannot be read or has not been read yet for this conversation: no icon rather than a wrong "not in Contacts". The same icon in the text and the HTML readers; its tooltip (and VoiceOver) says which, and a click on it does what `ctrl-c` does for that message. A card created or changed in Contacts while the mail is open shows at once: the app reads the cards again when macOS says the book changed (`CNContactStoreDidChange`).
- **`ctrl-c`**: on the sender of the current message (the one `ctrl-k` / `ctrl-j` or a click on a header made current, else the last unfolded one; a message of the user's own stands for the latest mail received before it), opens their card in Contacts when they have one, and otherwise copies their address to the clipboard (to create the card, or write to them). A book that cannot be read copies too. **`ctrl-C`** (with Shift) lists the mails of that sender from every folder, as Return on the contacts screen does (`contact: …` in the status bar; Escape goes back to the folder); in one column, the list comes back in place of the mail.
- **Access**: macOS asks the first time `l` or `ctrl-c` is pressed, never at launch. Refused, the panel shows the mail's links alone and the status bar says once per session where to allow it (System Settings, Privacy & Security, Contacts). Once allowed, the cards are read with the thread, so they are there when `l` opens.
- **Installed app only**: macOS asks only an app whose `Info.plist` says why (`NSContactsUsageDescription`, written by `make bundle` in English, and in each other language of the app from `apps/NiceMailer/Bundle/<language>.lproj/InfoPlist.strings`). `make app` runs a bare executable: it does not read Contacts and says so once.
- **Nothing is copied**: the cards are read on demand and kept for the session; nothing goes into NiceMailer's database or to the core. The lookup searches the cards' addresses (Contacts' own index), whatever their case, away from the main thread (`AddressBook.swift`).
- **Tests**: `AddressBookTests` (rows of a card, `tel:` URLs, senders, the question asked from `l` only, a refusal said once; the icon of each sender, `ctrl-c` opening a card or copying, a click on the icon, a card added in Contacts, `ctrl-C`), `addressbook.uiscript` and `sendercard.uiscript` use a book of their own (`FixtureAddressBook`): the user's cards are never read, and macOS's real question is not covered by automated tests.

### Calling a number

Return on a phone number of `l` (the mail's numbers, the senders' cards), or a click on a `tel:` link of the HTML view, hands the number to macOS, which calls it with the iPhone (FaceTime, Continuity). macOS does not call at once: it shows a **notification** with a green button, a phone icon and no text. A notification never takes the keyboard focus, so Return cannot press it; macOS offers no setting nor API to call without it (a protection against pages and apps calling numbers behind the user's back).

`ui.auto_confirm_calls = true` in `config.toml` makes the app press that button itself, through the accessibility API (the one VoiceOver uses), so that Return on a number calls. Off by default. The app applies a change of the file at once.

- **Permission**: the first call asks macOS to show its dialog; NiceMailer must then be allowed in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility. Until it is, the call is only opened and the status bar says where to allow it. The permission is broad (it lets the app drive the interface of any app), which is why the setting is off by default. macOS grants it to an app by its signature: with `make app` (a bare executable) it is the terminal that runs it that must be allowed, with `make bundle` NiceMailer itself.
- **What is pressed**: a button, or a notification action, whose accessibility label is the one macOS gives the call action in its own translations (`TelephonyUtilities.loctable`, `DIAL_CALL_…_OSX`: "Call", "Appeler", "Call %@ Using iPhone"…, every language of the system), as a whole label: "Rappeler" (call back, a missed call) is not "Appeler". It must have **appeared after** the number was opened: the buttons on screen before are noted first and never pressed. It is looked for every 100 ms for 5 s in the windows of the notifications, FaceTime and the older alert process, off the main thread (half a second at most per call to another process), so the window never freezes.
- **Nothing found**: nothing is pressed, the user clicks as before, and the status bar says "Call not confirmed: click the green button". The accessibility tree that was searched (the elements of the notifications on screen, their labels and actions) is written to `call-confirmation.txt` in the data directory, to adapt the search to another version of macOS. It may hold the texts of the other notifications shown at that moment; it stays on the Mac and is replaced by the next failure.
- **Found**: the status bar says "Calling…".
- **Code**: `CallConfirmation.swift` (labels, matching on any accessibility tree, the live search), `AppModel.openLink`.
- **Tests**: `CallConfirmationTests` (labels in two languages, whole labels only, a custom action of a notification, a button without text, only a new button, bounded search, the description written on failure, the labels of the running Mac) and `OpenLinkTests` (a call opened only by default, confirmed when asked, links that are not calls, the messages, the setting read from the core); `calls.uiscript` plays Return on a number with the setting on (`fixtures/calls.config.toml`), `pickers.uiscript` checks it is off by default. The press itself is not covered by automated tests: it would place a real call.

### Attachments

At the foot of each unfolded mail, its attachments are tiles: the icon of the file type (an SF Symbol in the theme's colours: image, PDF, text, spreadsheet, slides, archive, audio, video, code, calendar, contact, mail, other file), the name cut in the middle to keep its extension, and the size. A row holds 3 to 5 tiles, depending on the width of the reading pane. A folded mail shows a paperclip and the number of its attachments.

- A click saves the file to `~/Downloads`, never overwriting a file (`devis (2).pdf`); the status bar says where it went.
- ⌘-click saves it the same way, then opens the saved copy with its default app, in front.
- `l` lists the links of the mail, then one row per attached file, with its icon; Return saves the file, ⌘-Return saves it and opens the saved copy at once, in front. In a code mail (a site's or an app's one-time code: "Votre code de vérification : 610731"), the very first row is the **verification code**, with a lock and "Verification code" on the right: Return (or ⌘-Return) copies it, and the status bar says "Code 610731 copied"; typing "code" finds it. In a thread, it is the code of the latest message that gives one, since a newer code replaces the older ones. The core finds the code (`docs/core-library.md`, "Verification codes"). Then comes **Link to this mail**: Return copies a Markdown link to the mail, `[subject](nicemailer://m/<key>)`, ⌘-Return its bare URL; the link names the mail, not the database, so it opens on any Mac that has it, and in a conversation it is the link of the message the reader is on (ctrl-k / ctrl-j), which opening the link shows again (`docs/deep-links.md`). Next come the **senders' cards** from the Mac's address book (below). Then the rows come in groups: links with a text first ("Voir ma facture"); then the **phone numbers** of the mail, with a phone icon, which Return calls with the Mac's phone app (macOS opens `tel:` links with it; see "Calling a number" below); then the attached files; last, the links without a text, mostly an image leading to a social network or a shop, shown by their site (`www.facebook.com`). Each row is in two halves of the same width: on the left what the link is (its text, the site, the number), on the right, muted, where it goes in full (the whole address, the address of a `mailto:`, the number behind "Nous appeler") or the size of a file, so that a long text never hides the address. The panel is as wide as the window allows (a margin of 40 points on each side, 1,100 points at most at the default text size), so that long link texts and addresses can be read; what still does not fit is cut in the middle. Typing filters on the text and on the whole address, even the part not shown. A link the mail gives several times (a title, a picture, a button, or several tracking addresses to the same page) is one row, named by its first text and followed by how many other texts lead there: `ressent "les angoisses" du pays (and 2 others)`; typing one of these other texts finds it. For a tracking link, the right half shows the page it leads to when its address says so, rather than the tracker; it still opens through the tracking address. A phone number written in national form (`06 12 34 56 78`) is dialled as written; the core's rules for finding numbers are in `docs/core-library.md`, "Links of a message".
- `sa` saves every attachment of the marked mails (or of the one under the cursor), `sg` all but the graphics.

Images the HTML of the mail shows (`cid:`) are part of the body, not tiles. An image marked inline that no HTML shows (Apple Mail puts photos in a plain text mail this way) is a tile. The tiles follow the body in the HTML view too.

Files are extracted by the core from the raw message on first use (`docs/core-library.md`, "Attachments"). Generated mail (`make seed`) has no raw message: its tiles show, but saving one reports that the content is not available.

### Remote images

Most HTML mails show images stored on the sender's servers. Loading one tells the sender that the mail was opened, when, and from which IP address; many mails also hold a **tracking pixel**, an invisible image whose only use is that. NiceMailer never loads them unless asked.

- **Tracking pixels are removed by the core**, when the mail arrives: a remote image of 2 pixels or less (`width`, `height` or its style), or hidden by its style (`display: none`, `visibility: hidden`, `opacity: 0`), loses its source and is never requested, whatever the display. Their number is shown on the title line by an eye struck out and the number (its tooltip and VoiceOver say "pixels blocked: N"). On a mailbox of 46,539 mails, 63% of the mails had remote images, and a script applying close rules found 26,868 pixels in 58% of those.
- **Other remote images keep their address** in a form the web view cannot load by itself (`nm-remote:`). The `images` word of the switch appears only when the conversation has some.
- **`images`** (a click, or `vi`) shows the HTML with those images. The app downloads each one once, over HTTPS only (an `http:` address is upgraded, never sent in clear), without cookies, `Referer` or a revealing User-Agent, never towards this Mac or the local network, at most 10 MB and only `image/…` content. Images are kept in `~/Library/Caches/NiceMailer/RemoteImages` (file names are hashes: no address appears), so reopening a mail sends no new request. Only **unfolded** messages load their images.
- **Remembered for the sender, applied to authenticated mail only.** Choosing `images` remembers it for the sender, but the next mails load their images by themselves only when they pass authentication (`pass`, see `authentification-expediteur.md`): anybody can write in the name of a trusted sender, and loading their images would confirm that the address is read. For any other mail (imported mail, whose verdict is unknown, included), the thread opens as HTML; choosing `images` loads its images **for this conversation only**, and the status bar says why.
- **Never in Junk, Quarantine or Banned**, even when asked.
- **A second barrier inside WebKit.** The HTML view also carries a content rule list that blocks every `http`, `https`, `ws`, `wss` and `ftp` resource of the page (images, style sheets, fonts, scripts, media, fetches), should a remote source ever pass the core's sanitization. If WebKit refuses the list, the page is still shown (the core's sanitization being the first barrier) and the refusal is written once to the system log (`log stream --predicate 'subsystem == "NiceMailer"'`). `RemoteBlockerTests` checks that the list compiles and that a page with raw `http://` sources sends no request to a local server.
- `h` (or `vh`) goes from HTML or images to text; `vi` toggles between HTML and images.

What this does not hide: the IP address and the time of the first opening, for each image loaded (only a VPN changes that); a mail whose every image carries a token proper to the recipient (about a quarter of the mails with remote images) reveals the opening as soon as its images load; a pixel hidden by a parent element, or declared without any size, is not recognised. A message unfolded by a click inside the HTML page (rather than from the keyboard) shows its images only once the conversation is reopened. Images inside the mail itself (`cid:`) are not shown yet.

### Unsubscribing

When the latest received mail of the conversation says how to unsubscribe from its list, a button appears on the title line, left of `text | html | images` (never in Junk, Quarantine or Banned: unsubscribing from a spam confirms the address to the spammer; `unsubscribe.in_banned = true` in `config.toml` shows it in Banned, to clean that folder of the lists once signed up to, and the core then accepts it, [configuration.md](configuration.md)). Its look says where the link comes from and what a click does:

| Button | Link | A click (or ⌘U) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `unsubscribe` | `List-Unsubscribe` header, one click (RFC 8058) | the core unsubscribes at once; the status bar confirms, and the button gives way to "unsubscribed <day>" |
| `unsubscribe` with ↗ | header, a page of the list | opens the page in the browser |
| `unsubscribe` with an envelope | header, an address only | opens the unsubscribe mail in Mail (until NiceMailer sends mail) |
| `unsubscribe` with a link symbol | a link found in the text of the mail | opens it in the browser |

The button shows its key first, in the accent colour (`⌘U unsubscribe`), as every button of the title line (below). A one-click attempt the server refuses opens the page instead, and says why. The tooltip of the button tells the same. Only a one-click unsubscribe is recorded ("unsubscribed <day>"): after a page or a mail, the app cannot know whether you finished. The sender's decision and its mails are not changed. The links are found by the core when the mail arrives (`docs/core-library.md`, "Unsubscribing from a list").

**The flag button and the buttons of the title line** (added in November 2026). Between unsubscribe and `text | html | images`, a **flag** button says whether the conversation shown has a flag and adds or removes it: off, a hollow flag; on, the button filled with the selection colour and the flag solid, orange as in the list. No word on the button, as a macOS toggle: the solid flag says it is on. A click acts on the mail shown alone, even with other rows marked (which `I` would flag); its tooltip says what the click will do and recalls `I` (`AppModel.toggleFlag(of:)`). Unsubscribe, the flag and the commands of a reply (send, attach…, and the Bcc button) share one design (`FramedButton`, `FramedControls.swift`): a thin frame rounded as the rows of the list for its size (square in geek), the same room inside and the same height, a fill while the pointer is over it, and a tooltip (the word for unsubscribe, without its key on the button; the action buttons are their symbol alone, the key in the tooltip). The `text | html | images` switch is a group of segments in one frame of the same height, as a macOS segmented control (`FramedSegments`): the current segment is filled, so is the one under the pointer, and each tooltip recalls `h` (or `vh`) or `vi`.

### When a mail becomes read

- A mail you select (`j` `k`, a click, the next mail after a move) is marked read once shown for one second. Browsing quickly with `j` / `k` marks nothing. With one column the list alone shows no content, so staying on a row marks nothing; opening the mail (Return, double-click) marks it read at once, and inside the open mail `j` / `k` follow the one-second rule.
- The mail selected for you, the first one when a list opens or the one that replaces a mail that left the list, is not marked read by itself. It is marked read when you move from it to another mail of the list after at least one second. Moving it (`d`, `m`) or changing folder does not mark it.
- In a filtered list (unread, flagged…), the selected mail that stops matching (read after one second, `u`, `U`, `I`) stays under the cursor, shown with its new state, until you leave it; it then leaves the list. As in Gmail's unread view: without this, a list of unread mails would empty itself, one mail per second.
- A mail of **Quarantine** is never marked read by viewing it, by any of the rules above, wherever it is listed (Everything too): you are judging an unknown sender, not reading its mail, and the unread dot keeps saying which mails still wait for a decision. See "Quarantine" below.

### Copying a mail

`sc` asks the core for the text of the mail (`message_text`, `docs/core-library.md`) and puts it on the clipboard. A text body often holds line breaks its writer never typed: the sender's mail app cut each paragraph every 72 characters or so (format=flowed, or plain hard wrapping), and HTML conversions pile up empty lines. Pasted as is into a document, each paragraph would become a column of short lines. The core joins these pieces back, so that a paragraph is one line, and leaves one empty line between paragraphs. What the writer did mean stays as written: quotes (`>`, each depth on its own), the signature after `-- `, list items, indented lines (code, addresses), tables (tabs), short lines (a greeting, a name), and blocks where every line ends a sentence. Where it cannot tell, it keeps the line break: a line too many is easier to fix than two paragraphs glued together.

### Saving a mail as PDF

Two PDFs, of one mail of every marked conversation (or of the one under the cursor): in the conversation shown, the message the reader is on, as for every row of `s` (see `s` + key above). The capital saves, then opens the file in front (Preview), as `sT` does. The app asks the core for the record of the mail (`message_record`, [core-library.md](core-library.md), "Saving a message"), read from the raw message the server delivered, and lays it out itself (`MailPDF.swift`), since only it has the text layout and fonts of macOS. Both are A4, set in Helvetica, and share their first part:

- **the subject in bold**, as the title;
- a block with **every party**, one address per line, the name as written in this very mail in bold and the address beside it: the author(s), `Sent by` (the `Sender` header) and `Reply-To` when they differ from the author, `To`, `Cc`, `Bcc` (only known for a mail the user sent); then **sent on** (the `Date` header) and **received on** (the server's reception date), both to the second with the time zone, and the number and size of the attachments; a thin rule closes it;
- **the body**, whole, quotes (muted) and signature included; only the line breaks the sender's mail app inserted every 72 characters are removed (`message_text`), never a word;
- **the attachments**: name, type and exact size.

**`sp` (`P`), as PDF**, for every day: that is all, and the foot of each page holds only "Page n / N", centred.

**`si` (`I`), as PDF with technical information**, the last row of the menu, to produce in a dispute or before a court, adds:

- the **SHA-256 of each attachment**;
- **technical information**: Message-ID, In-Reply-To, References, the `Date` header as written (with the sender's time zone), the size and the **SHA-256 of the original message**, then the trace headers as written (`Return-Path`, every `Received`, `Authentication-Results`, `DKIM-Signature`, `ARC-*`, `X-Mailer`), and a sentence that says how to check the PDF against the original `.eml`. The account and folder, the app's authentication verdict and the date of the export are left out: they describe the user's mailbox, not the mail;
- **at the foot of every page**: "Page n / N" and the SHA-256 of the original message: a page taken out of the document still says which mail it belongs to, and that pages are missing;
- the hash in the document information of the PDF.

This PDF is a faithful, readable copy; the proof itself is the original message, with the DKIM signature of the sending domain. Save it too with `se`: `shasum -a 256 <file>.eml` gives the hash printed in the PDF. A mail without raw file (fake mail of `make seed`, or a body not downloaded yet) is printed from the database: the PDF says, in red, that it cannot be checked against the original.

In both, a heading or a label never ends a page without what follows it. The identifiers and hashes are set in Menlo, where `0` and `O`, `1` and `l` differ. The colours are fixed (black on white even in dark mode), and characters Helvetica lacks (Chinese, emoji) are drawn with a fallback font, never dropped. The labels and dates follow the app's language. The file is named as the other exports, `<date> <subject>.pdf`, and never overwrites one (` (2)`…).

On a screen, `j` `k` move, `ctrl-d` `ctrl-u` move by half a page, and Escape goes back to the mails.

On the Tags and Groups screens (`specifications/ui.md`, "Écrans Tags et Groupes"), `r` opens a panel on the current name, selected (typing replaces it); Return renames. The name of another tag or group, without case, is refused on the panel's row, as the core refuses it; a change of case is a rename. `d` asks first (`y` deletes, Escape keeps), with the tag's mails or the group's members. A tag is renamed or deleted in every account that has one of that name. Renaming changes the displayed name only, the IMAP keyword stays; deleting takes the tag off every mail, on the server too. A deleted group leaves its mails as they are. The attribute chips and the list of the tag or group follow the new name, or go; the open mail's header is read again (`AppModel.renameLabel`, `deleteLabel`, `labelChanged`).

### Pickers

Pickers (`@`, `l`, `t`, `g`, `f`) are filterable lists: type to filter, ignoring case and accents; the best matches come first (the label starts with the typed text, then each typed word starts a word of the label, then initials such as `nf` for "not flagged", then each word anywhere in the label; see "Reaching a row from the keyboard" above), `ctrl-n` / `ctrl-p` or the arrows to move, Return to choose, Escape to close. A detail at the right end of a row is either a count to notice (unread mails, in the unread colour; a warning such as "nothing in between") or an information (where a link goes, how a date was read, "domain"), muted. A picker opens on its first row and turns around: up (or `ctrl-p`) from the first row selects the last one, so the end of the list is one key away, and down (or `ctrl-n`) from the last row comes back to the first.

The typed text is edited as in any macOS text field (`LineEdit.swift`): ← and → move the cursor, ⌥← ⌥→ by word, ⌘← ⌘→ (or Home, End, `ctrl-a`, `ctrl-e`) to either end; Delete removes the character left of the cursor, forward delete (fn-Delete) the one right of it, ⌥-Delete a word, ⌘-Delete everything before the cursor. **⌘V** pastes the text copied in any app at the cursor, as one line: line breaks become spaces and the spaces around it go (copying a word often takes the space after it). The contacts filter (`/` on the contacts screen) is edited the same way.

A click outside an open picker, prefix menu or the help closes it, like Escape. The click does nothing else: the mail under it is not selected. A click on a block of the status bar opens that block's menu or picker directly. A question (a suspect move, a decision) is not closed by a click: it waits for its answer.

## Moves and decisions

Moving a mail to Newsletter, Paperwork or Banned, or out of Quarantine to the Inbox, also decides where its sender's next mails go and files the mails already received from it (`folder-workflow.md`). The message says so: "“Promo” moved to Newsletter · next mails of news@shop.fr too · 12 other mails filed · z: undo".

`z` undoes the last move or decision: the mails go back where they were and the previous decision returns. Each `z` goes one step further back (50 steps, for the session); a move of several marked mails is one step. A mail moved again since stays where it is. The cursor goes back to the conversation brought back (the first of the list, for several): the move had taken it to the next one, where a second Delete after `z` would have hit another conversation (`Workflow.cursorAfterUndo`).

When the sender's mail is suspected of spoofing, the core refuses the move and the app asks, in a small menu (mode `ASK`) with the mail under the cursor: `y` moves it anyway, `t` moves it and accepts this sender's mail without authentication from now on (a sender whose tool is badly configured), Escape leaves it where it is. Every other key waits for the answer. Moving such a mail to Banned concerns that mail only and asks nothing.

The same small menu chooses a decision on the contacts screen (`m`, `M`); the current decision is marked "•". Keys, the arrows with Return, or a click choose.

### Quarantine

In Quarantine the only thing to do with a mail is to decide where it goes, so the move targets are single keys:

- **The status bar shows the targets** at its right end, just before `?`, in place of the `m` and `s` blocks: the move arrow, then each target with its key, `Inbox i  Newsletter n  Paperwork p  Junk x  Banned b  Deleted d` (the core's `move_targets` for the folder of the mail under the cursor). On the right they stay in the same place whatever the account, the folder and the chips on the left; a message (the result of a move) comes before them and is cut first when the bar is short. A click on one moves as its key does.
- **One key moves**: `i` does what `mi` does elsewhere, for the marked mails or the one under the cursor. The cursor goes to the next mail, as after any move, and `z` undoes. `d` moves to **Deleted** here (Archive is not a target from Quarantine), while it archives everywhere else.
- **`m`** opens no menu: the status bar says which keys to press.
- **Refused**: `u` `U` (read state), `t` (tag), `g` (group), `r` `R` (reply: writing to the sender would accept it), `F` (forward: sort the mail first) and `s` (save: its block leaves the status bar; the mail is to be sorted, not kept). The status bar says "In Quarantine, a mail can only be moved or flagged".
- **Still available**: `I` (flag), the navigation and marks, `v` (display), `l` (links), `z`, Delete, and the keys of every screen.
- **Never read by viewing**: see "When a mail becomes read". The core itself does not refuse `set_thread_seen`: the CLI can still mark a mail of Quarantine read when asked to.
- **Person or robot before the name**: the sender column starts with an icon, a silhouette (in the accent colour) when a person wrote the mail named, a gear (muted) when a robot did (`senders.md`). Among the robots of Quarantine, a person is the mail most likely to deserve the Inbox. The icon is the verdict of that very mail (`ThreadSummary.correspondent_kind`): a bulk mail is a robot even from a person's address. A mail without verdict (no readable sender, catch-up not done) keeps a blank place, so the names stay aligned; the icon takes its room from the name, never from the other columns. VoiceOver reads it with the name ("Alice, written by a person"). Forcing the origin of the address on the Contacts screen (`o`) reloads the list, and the icon follows.

This holds while a Quarantine folder is listed (of an account, or of "all"). In Everything, or a tag, group or contact list, the rows come from several folders: the `m` and `s` blocks and the usual keys stay (`InterfaceController.directTargets`).

## Implementation notes

- Keys are read by an AppKit event monitor (`InterfaceController.handle`) before any view, so a key is never lost to a focused field: picker text is edited by the controller itself, with its cursor in a `LineEdit` value (pure functions, tested on their own), and ⌘V is read there too. AppKit gives the arrows and other function keys characters of the private use area (U+F700–U+F8FF): `LineEdit.isTypable` rejects them, otherwise the left arrow would type an invisible character.
- All user-facing strings go through `L(...)` and the String Catalog `Resources/Localizable.xcstrings` (English and French). The language is chosen explicitly from the user's preferences, because an app launched with `swift run` has no main bundle declaring its languages.
- Rows of lazy lists receive plain values (cursor, marks) as parameters: reading the observable model only inside a `ScrollView` closure did not always refresh them.
- Clicks use tap gestures filtered by modifier (`View.onClick`): SwiftUI runs tap actions after the click was processed, when `NSApp.currentEvent` no longer describes it.
- The core's events invalidate folders and threads; the reloads they cause are paced (`AppModel.scheduleRefresh`, `evenements.md`, section 7): the first event makes a reload due, the next ones add to it; it starts after 40 ms, at least 500 ms after the previous one started (`refreshDelay`, a pure function), and never while another runs. During an import the events never pause: the lists are still reloaded, twice a second at most, and never pile up in the core in front of what the user waits for (`RefreshPacingTests`).
- A mail is listed and can be opened as soon as its headers are in; the reader then says "(body not downloaded)", and opening it puts its body first in the download queue. The body's arrival is announced by a `MessageChanged` alone (no `ThreadChanged`), so the app reads it too: for the open thread, the reader is reloaded and shows the body without the user leaving the mail; for a listed thread, its list is reloaded (the row's snippet comes with the body); for any other thread, nothing is reloaded, so the bodies of a whole account coming in cost nothing beyond the mails on screen (`AppModel.messageDirties`, `BodyArrivalTests`).
- The reader loads the selected conversation with at most one `get_thread` in flight (`AppModel.loadOpenThread`): a move made while one loads only records the new selection, which is loaded once the first answer is in. Holding `j` used to ask for every mail passed over; the requests queued behind the core's six reader connections, and the mail finally under the cursor, and the lists behind it, came seconds later (`core.log`: `slow request: get_thread took 9630 ms (9500 ms waiting for the database…)`).
- A status bar message computed after a wait (the reply screen opening, which waits for `list_senders`) is shown only if nothing newer was said meanwhile (`AppModel.sayOpening`, `flashCount`): Escape pressed in between keeps its own message.
- Tested by `Tests/NiceMailerTests` (unit) and `UITests/*.uiscript` (scripted UI); see `docs/testing.md`.
